# Research: what makes a strong YC company website

The study behind this site's design, done on 14 October 2026.

## Method

1. Pulled the full YC company directory (6,297 companies, from ycombinator.com/companies via the yc-oss mirror of its data).
2. Searched it for the categories closest to MechLens: smart glasses, wearables, heads-up displays and AR, plus YC's best-known hardware companies.
3. Opened 58 company sites in a browser at 1440 × 900 and captured the first screen and the full page. Read the computed styles: headline typeface, size, tracking, colours, animation libraries.
4. Walked the strongest nine screen by screen, scrolling like a visitor so every reveal plays: Mentra, AirCaps, Subvocal, Fort, Boom, Astranis, Oklo, Stripe and E1O.

## What the best hardware sites do

1. **The headline names the product and who it is for, in under eight words.** "AI Glasses for Frontline Workers" (Mentra). "Talk without talking." (Subvocal). "The Strength Wearable" (Fort). No slogans that need decoding.
2. **The product is the hero image.** AirCaps and Subvocal put the glasses alone on a light, seamless background; Mentra shows them on a worker; Boom fills the screen with the aircraft. The product is never replaced by an illustration or a diagram of the software.
3. **A product reveal follows the hero.** The product name, one line on what it does, and the product large and centred (Mentra Live, AirCaps).
4. **Three to four capabilities, each tied to a job.** "Document work while it happens", "Call an expert anywhere", "Guide the next step" (Mentra). Short headings, one sentence each.
5. **A trust section.** Mentra's "Sovereign AI, private data, full ownership"; AirCaps' accuracy and privacy claims. For a product that gives answers, trust is the purchase decision.
6. **A section per buyer.** AirCaps splits by audience; Mentra pairs Buy with Talk to Sales for companies.
7. **FAQ, then one final call to action.** "Good questions." then "Be the first to get it." (Subvocal); waitlist pages ask for an email and little else (Fort).
8. **Light backgrounds, regular-weight headlines, pill buttons.** Mentra (Red Hat Display), AirCaps and Subvocal (Inter) all set large headlines at regular or medium weight with tight tracking, on white or light grey, with rounded pill buttons.
9. **Motion is rare.** Most of these sites are static apart from product media. The ones that animate do it around the product (a rotating model, a video), not on every section.

## Patterns left out

- Logo strips, press quotes and customer numbers: MechLens has none yet, and inventing them would be the first thing a reviewer catches.
- A "Backed by Y Combinator" badge: MechLens is not in YC.
- Scroll-driven, pinned storytelling (Dawn, E1O): memorable on those sites, but it slows a product page down and was ruled out for this one.
- Identical icon-and-title card grids (Parrot, Rodeo): they read as a template.
- Software internals on the main page: no model names, scores or benchmarks.

## How this site applies it

| Finding | On the MechLens site |
|---|---|
| Category plus audience headline | "AI goggles for automotive technicians." |
| Product as the hero | A 3D model of the goggles, built in code, on a light studio background; drag to turn it |
| Product reveal with parts | "Everything it needs is built in": camera, microphones, push-to-talk button, status light, each marked on the render |
| Job-based capabilities | "Look. Ask. Get the answer.": three steps beside the engine bay seen through the visor outline |
| Trust section | "Verified answers, or a clear “not sure.”": exact vehicle, the source with every answer, no guessed numbers, honest when unsure |
| A section per buyer | Technicians, and shops and dealerships, each with its own call to action |
| FAQ and one call to action | "Common questions." then "Be first to wear it." with the waitlist form |
| Light, regular weight, pills | Studio grey and white, Archivo at medium weight, yellow pill buttons |
| Little motion | One page-load entrance, the draggable model, the FAQ; nothing on scroll |
